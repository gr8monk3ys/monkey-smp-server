# What the most successful Minecraft servers do, and what Monkey SMP should take from them

*Researched 25 Nov 2026 for the Season 1 relaunch (Saturday 6 Feb 2027). Player counts are status pings from
public trackers, which a server can fake, so treat them as rough. Revenue figures for Minecraft servers are almost
never published; every money number here is either a store price, a company's own claim, or a third party's model,
and is marked as such. Fan wikis are used for game rules because the servers themselves don't document them; they
are marked "fan".*

This doc doesn't change anything in the other relaunch workstreams. Where a finding disagrees with the current plan,
it is listed under [Conflicts with the current plan](#conflicts-with-the-current-plan) for that workstream's owner
to decide.

## In short

- **The format is right.** PvP and survival are the two biggest categories by players online, and the second
  biggest server in the world, DonutSMP, is a free economy survival server with lifesteal PvP, which is what
  Season 1 is.
- **The one thing every winner shares is not selling power.** Hypixel and Wynncraft sell cosmetics only. DonutSMP
  lost more than half its players in one week of June 2026 after an update players saw as pay-to-win, alongside a
  database wipe with no backup. Mojang's 2014 rules that stopped Mineplex selling advantages are one of the causes
  usually given for its decline.
- **Growth comes from video, and a handful of creators bring most of it.** 2b2t's biggest wave followed one
  YouTube video; Wynncraft's player record came after a trailer; on Tebex, the top 1.6% of creators drive about
  three quarters of creator-code sales. DonutSMP gives a free rank to anyone whose videos pass a public view count.
- **Seasons work when the end is part of the show.** The Lifesteal SMP creator series that started the genre ends
  each season by destroying the map; 2b2t, which never resets, released its world as a download. A fixed end date
  and a finale give creators a story to film.
- **Bedrock is half the market.** The Hive (Bedrock only) reports 7 million players a month, and two of the five
  busiest servers today are Bedrock servers.
- **Keep backups off the server and test restoring them.** The single event that cost DonutSMP the #1 spot was
  partly a database loss.

## Conflicts with the current plan

These are for the owning workstream to decide. Nothing on their branches was changed.

1. **Rules/seasons, elimination.** The rules thread's draft keeps eliminated players out until someone revives them.
   The original Lifesteal SMP banned on the last heart too, but it was a private server of friends with teams.
   On a public server, a solo player with no team has no way back, and an eliminated player is a player who leaves
   and doesn't buy. *Inferred, not measured:* consider a timed return (for example 24 hours, back at a low heart
   count) beside the revive beacon, or a revive that a player can earn alone.
2. **Store, rank perks.** DonutSMP's paid rank includes priority queue, more homes, instant auction-house buys and
   shards earned anywhere. None of that should be copied: priority queue breaks Mojang's same-price-for-access rule,
   and the auction and shard perks are economy advantages. The plan already says cosmetic ranks only; this is a
   warning against borrowing DonutSMP's rank sheet.
3. **Platform, backups.** The repo's backup and restore docs describe the old modded server. As of 25 Nov the
   relaunch platform branch has no relaunch doc yet, so check that backups cover the Paper world and the economy
   and auction data, stored off the server, with a restore drill before the beta.
4. **Brand/marketing, map markers.** Already flagged by the rules thread: BlueMap and claim markers reveal bases,
   which kills the raid content that drives clips (idea 7).

## Server by server

### DonutSMP: the closest model, and the clearest warning

- **What it is.** A free Java server (fan wikis say Bedrock can join) with an economy, an auction house, buy orders,
  spawners that produce drops without mobs, teams, and lifesteal PvP where a kill steals a heart. Founded in 2021 by
  the streamer DrDonut (third-party; not confirmed at source). Its Discord passed 500,000 members by 2025
  (third-party).
- **Seasons.** Season 1 "ended due to an overinflated economy and duping"; Season 2 is billed as permanent (fan).
- **How it grew.** Creator videos: "100 days", economy tutorials, "richest player", base raids and traps
  (third-party). It gives a free Donut+ rank for 90 days to creators who pass public thresholds: 25 average live
  viewers, 100 on TikTok Live, 5,000 YouTube views or 50,000 short-form views, with the IP on screen and content
  from the current season (fan wiki quoting the support page).
- **Money.** Donut+ is a 30-day subscription at $5.49 (fan); Donut++ and +++ came in June 2026, and a vendor
  write-up lists $4.99, $9.99 and $14.99 a month. Shards, the second currency, are sold for real money ($0.99 to
  $99.99 per a vendor page) and, since June 2026, buy netherite gear and a max-enchanted mace in a shard shop and
  used to buy spawners (fan). Tebex told a vendor that DonutSMP's subscription revenue grew 340% in a year. MineRanks
  shows "$12,480 a month", but that is its own model from an assumed $8.99 average purchase, not a reported figure.
- **What went wrong.** DonutSMP beat Hypixel on weekly average every week from January to late May 2026 (44,400 to
  27,648 in the week of 19 Feb). The week of 4 June it fell from 28,020 to 13,076 (minecraft-stats, 2 Jul 2026).
  Community reports blame two things at once: a June update that removed AFK and kill shards, the shop and crates
  and moved shards to real money only, which players called pay-to-win (a Change.org petition followed), and a
  staff mistake that deleted the database proxy and erased stats, inventories, money and ranks with no usable
  backup. Its last 30 days are still 30% below the 30 days before (MineRanks).
- **Take:** the loop (sell, auction, buy orders, raid, steal hearts), the media rank and the creator focus. **Don't
  take:** a sellable second currency, spawners or gear for money, paid queue or perks, a "permanent" season.

### Hypixel: cosmetics only, for 13 years

- Started 13 Apr 2013; SkyWars 2015, BedWars 2017, SkyBlock 2019 (vendor history). The founder says the Java server
  has seen 38,717,739 unique players and that about half of everyone who bought Java played on it once (GamesRadar,
  29 Jul 2025). Today it is #1 again by players online.
- Sells ranks (VIP to MVP+), MVP++ as a subscription, and in-game cosmetics and boosters (Hypixel support page; prices
  aren't on it). A vendor history credits it with keeping cosmetic-only monetization when others didn't.
- SkyBlock adds a server-wide election where players vote for a "mayor" whose perks change the economy for a while
  (not read at source, see gaps): a cheap way to give everyone a reason to log in and argue.
- **Take:** cosmetic ranks plus a subscription, a long-running content cadence, and community votes with real
  stakes. **Not for us:** its scale of original game modes.

### The Hive and CubeCraft: Bedrock's giants

- The Hive started in 2013, became a Bedrock partner server in 2018 and closed its Java server in April 2021
  (HandWiki). It reports 7M+ monthly players (hivegames.io). It sells through the Bedrock Marketplace in Minecoins,
  for example a lifetime Hive+ rank at 1,690 Minecoins (MinecraftPal). CubeCraft has run since 2012 and joined Bedrock
  in 2018 (Mojang's own interview with its CEO, 25 Apr 2018).
- Featured Bedrock servers are an invite-only program with 11 operators (MinecraftPal), so Monkey SMP can't use the
  Marketplace. Consoles join third-party servers only through a DNS workaround (platform notes), so the Bedrock
  audience we can reach is mostly phones and Windows.
- A cautionary case: Pixel Paradise, a featured server, shut in August 2023 after charging for access to basic game
  modes (MinecraftPal). Free core play with cosmetics on top is what lasts there.
- **Take:** treat Bedrock players as half the audience, keep every mode free.

### Wynncraft: content and a trailer

- An MMORPG in Minecraft since 29 Apr 2013; Guinness named it the largest Minecraft MMORPG in 2017. Free, with
  cosmetic purchases only. Its player record of 10,716 came in February 2026 after the trailer for its Fruma
  expansion (Wikipedia). Tebex puts it at 150,000+ monthly players (vendor quoting Tebex).
- **Take:** a trailer timed before a launch moves the record. **Not for us:** a hand-built world that takes years.

### 2b2t: the story is the product

- Anarchy since December 2010, never reset, world over 100 TB. Its biggest wave followed TheCampingRusher's
  1 June 2016 video (two million views in four months), which also set off a war between old and new players
  (Wikipedia). Paid priority queue $20 a month (Wikipedia); Tebex says its subscription revenue grew 93.9% (vendor).
- In August 2023 a terrain reset and a "soft item economy reset" caused backlash, a rollback and refunds for
  priority buyers. In May 2026 it released a 24 TB world download (Wikipedia).
- **Take:** a big creator video can double a server overnight, so plan for it; announce resets long before; a
  world archive is content. **Don't take:** a paid queue (breaks Mojang's equal-access rule for us).

### Lifesteal servers and the series that started them

- The Lifesteal SMP creator series (Mapicc and ParrotX2, from 2020; fan wikis) set the rules most servers copy:
  10 starting hearts, 1 to 20 range, a ban on the last heart, crafted hearts, and from Season 2 a revive beacon
  built from rare items such as totems and netherite. Each season gets a new map and new mechanics, and finales
  destroy the world (Season 2 ended with over a thousand withers). Its fandom grew to about 2,000 fan stories by
  July 2024 (Fanlore).
- Public lifesteal servers today: Complex Gaming (~6,100, a multi-mode network), FreshSMP (~1,750), InsanityCraft
  (~2,000), lifesteal.net (~580), ManaCube (seasonal resets). Most of the big ones are networks with lifesteal as
  one mode, cross-play is common, and they sell ranks and crate keys (listing sites, self-reported counts).
- **Take:** the series' rules and the finale as an event. Our edge over most lifesteal servers is the economy and
  a cosmetic-only store, since many of them sell keys and ranks with gameplay value (a review site warns of it).

### Mineplex and Dream SMP: two ways to fall

- **Mineplex** held the Guinness record for concurrent players (34,434, January 2015), helped by a partnership with
  CaptainSparklez. A vendor history blames its decline on an ageing codebase, founders leaving, the 2014 rules that
  stopped it selling advantages, copying BedWars late, and cheaters. It shut without notice on 11 May 2023 citing
  "financial sustainability", and relaunched on Java in June 2026 (Wikipedia).
- **Dream SMP**, a private creator SMP, became one of the biggest stories on Twitch (a 650,000-viewer stream) on
  storylines and cross-channel arcs, and ended with its map blown up in November 2022 (Wikipedia).
- **Take:** build the business on cosmetics from day one, and give creators story beats, not just a map.

## Patterns across the winners

- **Format.** One clear loop a new player gets in a minute (sell, buy, fight), and a reason to come back
  tomorrow: an economy that moves, hearts to win back, a finale on the calendar.
- **Retention.** Seasons with a fixed end, finales as events, things that carry over (titles, cosmetics, Discord
  roles), and community decisions. MineGuard (vendor) suggests 2 to 3 month seasons for active servers, which the
  8 to 10 week plan matches.
- **Money.** Cosmetic ranks, a monthly subscription, a season pass, creator codes. The servers that sold power
  either lost players (DonutSMP 2026) or had to stop (Mineplex 2014).
- **Growth.** Creators first: a few big videos and a steady stream of short clips, with a free rank for creators
  who post. *Inferred:* server lists and voting help a small server be found, but none of the top servers' growth
  stories in these sources turn on them.
- **Failure.** Dupes and inflation, paid advantages, data loss, ageing code and absent owners.

## What Mojang allows, and what top servers charge

- Mojang's 2020 update to the commercial guidelines dropped the "cover your costs" rule ("okay with us if you want to
  run a business, within the rules"), made the test "a competitive gameplay advantage" or a worse experience for
  other players, and added a rule to disclose paid influencer deals (minecraft.net, 17 Apr 2020). The current
  guidelines allow selling cosmetics "except for capes or anything that attempts to visually act like the feature of
  a Minecraft player cape" (quoted by ConductAtlas from minecraft.net/usage-guidelines). The store thread has the
  full rule list, read at source.
- Prices seen: Donut+ $5.49 a month (fan) or $4.99 to $14.99 for three tiers (vendor); 2b2t priority $19.99 a month;
  ManaCube ranks $9.99 to $179.99; Hive+ 1,690 Minecoins; Hypixel MVP++ is a subscription (price not read).
- A creator code roughly doubles the basket on Tebex mobile stores ($27 to $52, Tebex's own data).

## What we couldn't verify

- Hypixel SkyBlock's mayor rules and coin taxes: the SkyBlock wiki refused automated reads.
- DonutSMP's official store, rules and support pages weren't read; its prices and media-rank rules come from fan
  wikis quoting them, and its June 2026 causes from "community reports".
- No source gives first-week retention numbers for a Minecraft server; one guide claiming "real data from 300+
  servers" showed none.
- Hypixel's and DonutSMP's revenue: nothing published; only vendor models.
- Hypixel's rank prices: not on its support page; the store wasn't read.
